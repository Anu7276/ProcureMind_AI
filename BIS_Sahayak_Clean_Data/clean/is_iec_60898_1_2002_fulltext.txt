=== STATUS CORRECTION (important for version/amendment tracking) ===
IS 8828:1996 was WITHDRAWN on 2008-01-11. It is superseded by TWO standards:
  - IS/IEC 60898-1:2002 (this document) - AC-only circuit-breakers
  - IS/IEC 60898-2:2003 - AC and DC circuit-breakers
Per BIS Connect record: IS 8828:1996, Reviewed 2006, Status "Withdrawn", superseded by
IS/IEC 60898-1:2002. This is exactly the kind of version/amendment-checker fact your
architecture diagram's "Version & Amendment Checker" module should catch - recommending
IS 8828:1996 today would be citing a dead standard.

=== SCOPE AND OBJECT (Clause 1) - KEY DISAMBIGUATION FROM IS/IEC 60947-2 ===
"This part of IEC 60898 applies to a.c. air-break circuit-breakers for operation at 50 Hz
or 60 Hz, having a RATED VOLTAGE NOT EXCEEDING 440V (between phases), a RATED CURRENT NOT
EXCEEDING 125A and a RATED SHORT-CIRCUIT CAPACITY NOT EXCEEDING 25000A. As far as possible,
it is in line with the requirements contained in IEC 60947-2."

"These circuit-breakers are intended for the protection against overcurrents of wiring
installations of buildings and similar applications; they are designed for USE BY
UNINSTRUCTED PEOPLE and for not being maintained. They are intended for use in an
environment with pollution degree 2. They are suitable for isolation."

Explicitly EXCLUDED from this standard's scope:
  - circuit-breakers intended to protect motors
  - circuit-breakers with user-accessible adjustable current setting

NOTE 1 in the standard itself: "For more severe overvoltage conditions, circuit-breakers
complying with other standards (e.g. IEC 60947-2) should be used."//

THIS IS THE EXACT DISAMBIGUATION RULE for a recommendation engine:
  - Rated current <=125A, voltage <=440V, Icu <=25000A, household/uninstructed-person use,
    non-adjustable settings -> IS/IEC 60898-1 (or -2 for DC)
  - Anything above those ceilings, OR industrial/instructed-person use, OR motor protection,
    OR adjustable settings -> IS/IEC 60947-2
  - Pollution degree 2 (60898-1) vs pollution degree 3 default (60947-2, per its Clause 6)
    is another distinguishing signal between the two standards.

Related standards referenced in scope: IEC 60898-2 (AC+DC circuit-breakers), IEC 61009-1/
-2-1/-2-2 (residual current circuit-breakers with integral overcurrent protection - RCBOs),
Annex D (co-ordination under short-circuit conditions with another SCPD).

=== KEY DEFINITIONS (Clause 3) - overlap and divergence from IS/IEC 60947-1/-2 ===
- circuit-breaker (mechanical) (3.1.4): same core wording as IEC 60947-1/-2 definition, with
  "automatically breaking currents" made explicit.
- protected pole / unprotected pole / switched neutral pole (3.2.7.x) - household-specific
  pole classification not present in Part 2.
- uninterrupted duty (3.2.14) - same concept as Part 1's 4.3.4.2.
- short-circuit (making and breaking) capacity (3.5.5): DIFFERENT formula basis than Part 2 -
  defined relative to "0,85 times its non-tripping current for the conventional time" rather
  than Part 2's Icu/Ics % table (Table 1).
- instantaneous tripping current + Type B/C/D classification (3.5.17, Clause 4.5) - THIS
  CLASSIFICATION DOES NOT EXIST IN IS/IEC 60947-2. Type B/C/D (tripping curve shape) is a
  household/MCB-specific concept - useful signal for a classifier distinguishing the two
  standards from free-text queries mentioning "Type B breaker" or "Type C MCB".
- Cross-references confirm shared lineage: total/partial discrimination, selectivity limit
  current, take-over current definitions are explicitly cited FROM IEC 60947-1 and
  IEC 60947-2 (3.5.14.1-3.5.14.9) - i.e. this household standard borrows its coordination
  concepts directly from the Part 1/Part 2 framework already in the dataset.

=== CLASSIFICATION (Clause 4) ===
4.1 By number of poles: single/two/three/four-pole (with protected/unprotected/switched-
  neutral pole combinations).
4.2 By protection against external influences: enclosed-type vs unenclosed-type.
4.3 By mounting: surface-type, flush-type, panel board (distribution board) type.
4.4 By connection method: fixation system (plug-in/bolt-on/screw-in) and terminal type
  (screw-type, screwless-type, flat quick-connect, aluminium-conductor screw-type).
4.5 By instantaneous tripping current - THE TYPE B/C/D CLASSIFICATION (Table 2):
    Type B: tripping range >3In to <=5In
    Type C: tripping range >5In to <=10In
    Type D: tripping range >10In to <=20In (up to 50In for special cases)
4.6 By I2t characteristic (optional manufacturer classification).

=== CHARACTERISTICS (Clause 5) ===
5.2 Rated quantities: Ue (rated operational voltage), Ui (rated insulation voltage - defaults
  to max Ue unless stated), Uimp (rated impulse withstand voltage per Table 3), In (rated
  current - reference ambient 30C), rated frequency, Icn (rated short-circuit capacity =
  ultimate short-circuit breaking capacity assigned by manufacturer).
5.3 Standard/preferred values:
  - Table 1: preferred rated voltages by pole count and system type (e.g. single-pole/
    single-phase 230V; three-pole/three-phase 400V; North American 120/240V variants)
  - Table 2 (5.3.2): preferred rated currents: 6,8,10,13,16,20,25,32,40,50,63,80,100,125A
  - Table 3 (5.3.3): standard frequencies 50Hz, 60Hz
  - 5.3.4: standard Icn values up to 10000A: 1500/3000/4500/6000/10000A; above 10000A up to
    25000A: preferred value 20000A (THIS IS THE HARD CEILING vs Part 2's much higher Icu
    ranges in its Table 2, up to and beyond 50kA)
  - Table 3 (5.3.6): rated impulse withstand voltage by installation nominal voltage (2.5kV
    for 120/240V single-phase mid-point-earthed; 4kV for 230/400V three-phase)

=== MARKING (Clause 6) ===
Required marking includes: manufacturer name/trademark; type/catalogue/serial number; rated
voltage(s); RATED CURRENT PRECEDED BY TRIPPING-TYPE SYMBOL (e.g. "B16", "C32", "D63") - this
compact marking format (letter+number) is a strong text-pattern signal for identifying
household MCBs in free-text queries; rated frequency (if single-frequency); rated
short-circuit capacity in amperes; wiring diagram; reference ambient temp if != 30C; degree
of protection if != IP20; for Type D: max instantaneous tripping current if >20In; Uimp if
2.5kV. Isolation suitability symbol (per IEC TC3 symbols). Terminal markings: N for neutral,
earth symbol per IEC 60417-5019.

=== STANDARD CONDITIONS FOR SERVICE (Clause 7) ===
- Ambient air temp: -5C to +40C (24h average <=+35C) - vs Part 2/Part 1 which don't state
  a universal range this explicitly at the product-standard level for LV switchgear generally.
- Altitude: <=2000m default.
- Pollution degree 2 (vs Part 2's default pollution degree 3, per Part 2 Clause 6) - THIS IS
  A CONCRETE DISTINGUISHING TECHNICAL PARAMETER between the household and industrial
  standards, useful as a retrieval/reranking feature.

=== CONSTRUCTION/OPERATION REQUIREMENTS (Clause 8) - selected ===
8.1 Mechanical design: trip-free mechanism required; multipole poles must make/break
  substantially together; switched neutral must not close after / open before protected
  poles; ON/OFF must be manually possible; isolating distance required in open position.
  8.1.3 Clearances/creepage (Table 4): detailed by insulating-material CTI group (Group I/II/
  IIIa/IIIb) and working voltage - same underlying framework as Part 1's insulation
  coordination definitions (2.5.46-2.5.58) but with household-specific numeric values.
  8.1.5 Terminals: Table 5 gives connectable copper conductor cross-sections by rated current
  (e.g. up to 13A: 1-2.5mm2; up to 125A: 25-50mm2).
8.2 Protection against electric shock: live parts must not be accessible via standard test
  finger (Fig 9); plug-in breakers must be replaceable without touching live parts.
8.3 Dielectric properties: power-frequency dielectric strength, isolating capability
  (verified via Table 4 item 1 clearances + tests), impulse withstand voltage.
8.4 Temperature-rise (Table 6): terminals 60K; external touchable parts (insulating) 40K;
  external metallic operating-means parts 25K; other external parts 60K.
8.6 Automatic operation - Table 7 (the core tripping-characteristic verification table,
  DIRECTLY analogous to Part 2's Table 6 but with household-specific multipliers):
    Test a: 1.13x In, cold, must NOT trip within 1h (In<=63A) / 2h (In>63A)
    Test b: 1.45x In, immediately after test a, MUST trip within 1h/2h
    Test c: 2.55x In, cold, must trip within 1s-60s (In<=32A) or 1s-120s (In>32A)
    Test d: Type-dependent instantaneous current (B:3-5In, C:5-10In, D:10-20In), must NOT
      trip within 0.1s
    Test e: Type-dependent instantaneous current (B:5In, C:10In, D:20In), MUST trip within
      0.1s
  8.6.2 Conventional non-tripping current = 1.13x In; conventional tripping current = 1.45x
  In (DIFFERENT multipliers than Part 2's 1.05x/1.30x - another concrete disambiguator).
8.7 Mechanical/electrical endurance testing.
8.8 Short-circuit performance: must make/break up to Icn at 105%(+/-5%) of rated voltage.
8.11 Resistance to abnormal heat and fire (glow-wire type test, referencing IEC 60695-2-10).
8.12 Resistance to rusting (ferrous parts).

=== TESTS (Clause 9) ===
Table 8 lists type tests: Indelibility of marking; reliability of screws/current-carrying
parts/connections; reliability of terminals; protection against electric shock; dielectric
properties/isolating capability; temperature-rise; 28-day test (indirect contact-overheating
check - UNIQUE to this household standard, not present in Part 2); tripping characteristic;
mechanical/electrical endurance; short-circuit; mechanical shock/impact; resistance to heat;
resistance to abnormal heat/fire; resistance to rusting.
Test conditions (9.2): ambient 20-25C, mounted vertically in free air on painted plywood
board, single-core PVC-insulated copper test cables per Table 9 (cross-sections by rated
current, e.g. 1mm2 for In<=6A up to 50mm2 for In=100-125A).
